1. The problem I would explore

Griffith's public Education and Student Experience Plan focuses on improving consistency, reducing complexity and helping students experience the University as a connected whole. That creates a useful transformation question:

How could a large multi-campus university make student support simpler and more consistent without immediately committing to a major technology replacement?

I would treat that as a hypothesis-led problem, not a pre-written business case. The first job is to understand whether handoffs, repeat demand, inconsistent guidance or unclear ownership are actually material - and where.

2. What I would test first

These are diagnostic questions to validate with service data, frontline interviews and student evidence. They are not claims about Griffith's current operating model.

Entry points

Are people entering through channels that make ownership unclear and create avoidable transfers?

Resolution

Are cases being closed administratively but generating repeat contact because the underlying issue is not resolved?

Knowledge

Do teams rely on different guidance, creating variation in answers and rework?

Digital suitability

Which high-volume, low-complexity demand is genuinely suitable for self-service - and which is not?

My starting measures would be deliberately small: first-contact resolution, transfer rate, repeat-contact rate and satisfaction.

3. How I would bring stakeholders to a decision

The workshop is not a brainstorming event. Its job is to turn evidence, operational knowledge and competing views into a decision that can be tested.

Before the room

Pre-wire the sponsor and critical stakeholders. Review available data, policy and constraints. Send a short pre-read that separates facts, assumptions and open questions.

In the room

Frame the decision, validate pain points, identify root causes, stress-test options and agree the smallest useful pilot.

After the room

Issue a decision log, actions and owners. Update the future-state view and turn unresolved items into analysis or delivery work.

A realistic facilitation challenge

Frontline: "We need more people."

Digital: "We should automate more."

Local teams: "Centralisation will lose context."

How I would reframe it

Before choosing capacity, automation or centralisation, what evidence would distinguish the three?

Bring the discussion back to demand, repeat contact, handoffs, complexity, service variation and constraints. The goal is not consensus for its own sake; it is a better decision.

4. Options and recommendation

I would keep the first executive choice simple and explicit.

A · Standardise

Common process definitions, clearer ownership and one controlled knowledge source.

Fast and lower risk, but may leave fragmented entry points.

Recommended first move

B · Standardise + common triage

Option A plus common intake/triage and targeted digital self-service for genuinely simple demand.

Large enough to test the operating model; small enough to change course.

C · Major platform transformation

Consolidate service technology and redesign processes around the new platform.

Potentially right later, but I would not make it the first decision without evidence.

Recommendation: prove the operating model first. Invest in larger technology only after the pilot shows where technology is genuinely the constraint.

5. A 12-week pilot to turn debate into evidence

Weeks 1-2Understand

Baseline, interviews and a simple current-state map.

Weeks 3-4Design

Triage, ownership, knowledge approach and pilot scope.

Weeks 5-7Prepare

SOPs, training, dashboard, issue log and readiness.

Weeks 8-10Pilot

Run, measure, listen and correct quickly.

Weeks 11-12Decide

Scale, iterate or stop based on evidence - not momentum.

First-contact resolutionShould improve if ownership and knowledge are clearer.
Transfer rateShould fall if triage works rather than adding another handoff.
Repeat-contact rateTests whether issues are actually resolved end-to-end.
SatisfactionGuardrail: efficiency is not success if the experience deteriorates.
